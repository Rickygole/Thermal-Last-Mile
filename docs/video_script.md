# Thermal Last Mile, four minute video script

Screen recording of the live application. You drive. No music bed, no logo, no team introduction.
Every figure is verified against pipeline output.

---

## 0:00 to 0:22 | The hook
*The Walk. Heat surface filling the frame.*

> Fourth of July, 2026. Houston. A Round of 16 match at NRG Stadium.
>
> Thirty-four degrees. And no wind at all. Three weather stations, all reporting dead calm at the same moment.
>
> Inside the stadium, a retractable roof and full air conditioning.
>
> Outside it, eleven hundred metres of open asphalt between your car and the gate.
>
> Nobody measured that walk. Not the city. Not the venue. Not the tournament.
>
> We did.

## 0:22 to 1:05 | What we actually modelled
*Scrub the hour control. Surface recolours. One brief oblique tilt.*

> We did not map temperature. Temperature does not tell you what a body absorbs.
>
> We modelled wet bulb globe temperature, the standard OSHA uses to stop outdoor work. Heat, humidity, wind and radiant load, together.
>
> One metre grid. Building shadows raytraced from real heights. Landsat surface temperature coupled in, so the field varies block by block.
>
> And then we walked it. Not averaged over a polygon. Integrated along the route, at one point three metres per second, the speed a person actually walks.
>
> Seven matches. Each one against its own observed weather, on its own date, with the shadows rebaked for that day's sun.

## 1:05 to 1:50 | The finding
*The Clock. Seven rows, then the comparison.*

> Here is what we expected to find. Hot blocks. Shade them.
>
> Here is what we found. There are no hot blocks.
>
> Across a hundred and seventy-two segments, the temperature spans less than one degree. The entire corridor is the hotspot.
>
> So shade cannot be targeted. But something else can.
>
> Six of Houston's seven matches kicked off at noon.
>
> That one decision cost fans three hundred and twenty thousand degree-hours of heat stress on the walk. Sixty-nine percent of the total.
>
> Every street tree this corridor can physically hold, about fifty thousand dollars of planting, buys back twelve thousand.
>
> The hour on the schedule is worth twenty-five times the trees. And it costs nothing.

## 1:50 to 2:35 | The control, and the number that moved
*Trip chart. 26 June selected.*

> One match did not kick off at noon. June twenty-sixth, seven in the evening.
>
> At the kickoff hour, it measured exactly zero. Same corridor. Same model. Same summer.
>
> But we did not stop there. Because nobody arrives at kickoff, and nobody stays after the whistle.
>
> So we modelled the whole trip. The arrival window, and the walk back out into the hottest part of the afternoon.
>
> And our own headline fell. Ninety-two percent became sixty-nine.
>
> Because the evening is not free either. That seven o'clock match carried twenty-one thousand seven hundred, once you count the walk in.
>
> We published the lower number. It is the one that answers the question.

## 2:35 to 3:20 | Three things we refused to claim
*Methods panel, organiser panel, equity panel.*

> The organisers gave us twenty-nine million rows of visitor data. We tested it against our heat field. The correlation was zero point one five. Too weak to mean anything. So we say that, instead of citing it as support.
>
> We built an evidence-based estimate of how fans arrive. Our own pipeline rejected it, because a car park has no shops in it to count. It falls back to a stated assumption, and says so on screen.
>
> Our equity analysis rests on three census tracts. Three. So we report that the design cannot answer the question, rather than quoting a correlation from three points.
>
> And our validation is one point five five degrees. That is the larger of our two figures. It is the one we quote.

## 3:20 to 4:00 | The close
*The Map, then the Transfer.*

> Everything here is measured against a tournament that already happened. That is more than a forecast can claim.
>
> A hundred and seventy-two segments. Ranked. Priced per degree-minute averted. Exportable as a spreadsheet a public works department can open on Monday.
>
> The kickoff hour belongs to the tournament. These segments belong to the city.
>
> Los Angeles has not set its hours yet. Twenty twenty-eight. Same pipeline, one afternoon.
>
> The walk from the platform to the gate is the part of a mega-event that nobody owns.
>
> Now it has a number.
